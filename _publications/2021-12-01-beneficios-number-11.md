The technological advances observed in recent decades provide countless benefits to society in general, although they bring with them potential risks when used by malicious agents aiming to commit crimes. The use of digital tools to commit offences has been termed cybercrime and has required profound changes in the legal world to face the challenges imposed on the investigation, collection of evidence, trial and application of sanctions to cybercriminals. Although several countries currently have their own legislation typifying conduct committed through computer systems, the complexity of the subject requires a more collaborative approach, through international cooperation between countries, with the aim of providing mutual assistance to the authorities responsible for investigating and trying these criminal conducts. Brazil, in recent years, has issued rules aimed at updating its legal system for this new digital reality, with a special focus on the legal provision of new crimes that can be committed over the Internet. However, the absence of regulation on procedural aspects and regarding the obtaining of electronic evidence stored in other countries has proved to be an obstacle to effective criminal prosecution. On the other hand, the Budapest Convention on Cybercrime has been in force since 2001, which aims to harmonize the legislation of signatory countries regarding various criminal conducts, in addition to providing rules for fast and efficient international cooperation, but to which Brazil has not yet adhered. Thus, this work aims to present some potential benefits of Brazil's accession to the Cybercrime Convention.
